Who this is for

Product managers, founders, operations leads, designers, analysts. Anyone whose job now expects them to show a working demo rather than describe one, and who has been waiting on an engineering team to find the time.

Job postings have started saying it in plain language. A recent Head of Product listing asked candidates to "prototype and iterate using AI-native tools to go from idea to working demo yourself." That expectation is now normal, and almost nobody teaches the skill.

You do not need to know how to code. You will not learn to code either. Half a day does not do that, and anybody promising it is selling something. You will learn to describe what you want, judge whether you got it, and recover when you did not.

What you will build

One small, finished, genuinely useful thing. You bring the idea, or I bring one from your world if you would rather not decide on the day.

It runs in a browser, it lives at a real URL, and you can send that link to a colleague or an interviewer the same afternoon. It is a prototype on purpose: it exists to make a decision cheaper, not to be shipped to customers.

What makes this different

You type. All of it.

Most training of this kind is a demonstration. You watch an expert move quickly, feel encouraged, and discover on Monday that you cannot reproduce any of it. Here you drive from the first minute, including the parts where it goes wrong. Especially those.

The failure playbook

These tools are fast, confident, and quietly wrong in ways that survive a casual look. I recently had one build a small timing tool: everything visible worked, it demoed beautifully, and the clock never actually started, so every number derived from it came out as zero.

Nothing in the code looked wrong. You catch that class of problem by knowing to ask, not by reading harder. That is the part of the day that transfers, and it is the part nobody else teaches.

You leave with the materials

A written reference sheet, a library of prompts that actually work, and the failure playbook itself. Yours to keep and to circulate internally.

Questions

What do I need before we start?
A laptop and about fifteen minutes of setup, which I will send in advance and help with if it gives you trouble. Most of the tools have a free tier that runs out quickly, so budget roughly $20 for the month if you plan to keep going afterwards.
Do I need to be technical?
No. It helps to be comfortable describing what you want precisely, which is a product skill rather than an engineering one.
Is there an age requirement?
Yes. The mainstream AI coding tools require users to be 18 or over in their own terms of service, so this is an adult workshop. For a parent and teenager who want to do it together on the parent's account, get in touch and we will work something out.
Can we do this for a whole department?
Not in one session. Eight is the ceiling for a single workshop because everyone has to be building. For larger groups, several sessions.
What if I already use these tools a bit?
Then skip the setup and tell me what keeps going wrong. The failure playbook is the half of the day that experienced users get the most from.
