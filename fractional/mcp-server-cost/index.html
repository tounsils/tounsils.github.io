What you are actually buying

Not a chatbot, and not a thin wrapper around your API. A production MCP server is a contract between your product and every AI assistant that calls it: a typed tool surface, grounding so the model never invents, a confidence level on every answer, and an evaluation corpus that decides whether the thing ships at all. The parts that cost money are the parts you can't see in a demo.

Every layer is visible in public source before you pay anything: ask-me-mcp is an MIT-licensed reference implementation of the exact pattern, installable in Claude in about two minutes.

The five drivers that move the number

DriverCheap endExpensive end
Tool surfaceFour read-only tools over one data sourceEight tools with writes, side effects, and approval flows
Existing APIYour product already has a clean API; the MCP server is one more storefront on itNo API exists, so the build includes carving one out of the application
GroundingOne well-structured internal sourceSeveral external sources, freshness requirements, and a domain taxonomy to encode
Evaluation depthSmoke evals per toolA certification corpus for a domain where a wrong answer costs real money
Deployment and authSingle-tenant endpoint, API-key authMulti-tenant, OAuth, observability, and connector-directory listing requirements

The second row is the one founders most often underestimate in their favor. If your product already exposes a real API, you are much closer to the cheap end than you think: the expensive half of the work already exists.

Three ways this gets priced in the market

  • Hourly or day rate. Open-ended by construction. The incentive runs against finishing, and the risk of scope growth is yours.
  • Platform subscriptions. Fine for generic connectors to common SaaS tools. They do not encode your domain, your grounding, or your release gate, because they can't.
  • Fixed fee against a defined done. What I sell. One quote after a fit check, six weeks, a gate per week, and a refund trigger: a live tool contract with a passing eval by the end of week six, or half the fee comes back.

Why no number on this page. A rate card invites comparison against work that is not the same work, and the right scope is worth establishing before the figure is. Ask on the call or by email and you get the number in one line, immediately. What is published is the shape: fixed, quoted once, six weeks, ships or half back.

What "done" means, concretely

  1. A remote MCP server deployed to a production endpoint with auth and observability.
  2. A typed tool contract your team can read, version, and extend.
  3. One passing eval on the production tool contract, run by an eval runner you keep.
  4. A deployable path to Claude's connector directory, ChatGPT's Apps SDK, and xAI tool calling. One server, three storefronts.

Anything short of that by the end of week six is a failed delivery and fires the refund trigger. The full week-by-week scope, gates, and exclusions are on the Playbook page.
